Calibrate the Claim

Practice matching the strength of a claim to what the evidence can support. You hedge the interpretation, never the data.

Pairs · about 35 min Sample table: 120 students, self-report
1 Hedge ladder 2 Match to evidence 3 Repair overclaims 4 Write a highlight

Hedge bank

Modal verbs

may, might, could

Reporting verbs

suggest, indicate, appear to

Adjectives

possible, probable, likely

Attribution and limits

respondents reported; the survey; self-reported; the sample

Boosters (use with care)

clearly, proves, cause

Hedge placement matters as much as hedge choice. "58% of EE students report difficulty" keeps the figure plain. "The data may show difficulty" hedges the number, which is the error to avoid.

Stage 1 · about 7 min

Hedge ladder

Six versions of one claim are mixed up. Put them in order from strongest to weakest, then label each one.

      Match hedge to evidence

      Read the table. For each claim, choose the minimum hedge the evidence requires. Self-report data and a sample of 30 per group limit how far a claim can go.

      Stage 3 · about 10 min

      Repair the overclaim

      Each sentence has a specific problem. Name the error, then rewrite the sentence so its strength matches the evidence.

      Stage 4 · about 10 min

      Write a calibrated highlight

      Write one highlight (1–3 sentences) about Table 3. Include one hedge, one attribution or limitation, and leave the figures plain. The checker updates as you type.
